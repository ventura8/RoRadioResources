RoRadio privacy policy

Last updated 30 September 2026. Applies to RoRadio for Windows (2.x) and RoRadio for Xbox and Windows 10 (1.x), published in the Microsoft Store.

RoRadio plays public internet radio streams. It has no account of its own, shows no advertising and contains no third-party advertising or tracking SDKs. This policy says exactly what leaves your device, why, and how to turn it off.

What RoRadio never collects

  • Your name, e-mail address, Microsoft account details, gamertag, machine name or Windows user name.
  • Your location.
  • Your IP address as part of diagnostics (the diagnostics service is configured with personally identifiable information disabled).
  • What you type: search text is never sent, only how many characters it had and how many results it found.
  • The names and addresses of stations you added — unless you turn that on yourself (see Stations you add).

Diagnostics (on by default, can be turned off)

To find crashes and stations that stopped working, RoRadio sends technical reports to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., trading as Sentry), which processes them on RoRadio's behalf. Transmission is over HTTPS. Each report carries:

Data Why
A random installation identifier (a GUID created on first run, stored on your device) To tell "one listener hit this 50 times" from "50 listeners hit this once", and to count how many installations run without a crash
App version and package architecture; Windows version, device family (desktop or Xbox) and device model To know which release and which systems a fault affects
Error type, message and stack trace when something fails To fix the defect
Playback quality: the station's identifier and stream format, whether playback started, how long it took to start, why it failed, how long you listened To repair or remove stations whose streams no longer work, and to keep start-up fast
Feature use: which pages are opened, whether favourites, search, the language picker and the controller are used (as counts, never as content) To decide what to improve and what to retire

The installation identifier does not name you, but it does single out a device, so this policy treats it as personal data. The lawful basis is our legitimate interest in a working, secure app; you can object at any time by switching diagnostics off.

How to turn it off: Settings › Send anonymous diagnostics. With it off, RoRadio starts no diagnostics session at all — nothing is queued, buffered or sent, including crash reports — and switching it off ends the session already running.

Stations you add

A station you add yourself can be a private or personal address, so its details stay on your device. Playback quality for it is counted anonymously (the app records only that the station was one of your own, with no name, address or description).

If you would like broken streams among your own stations to be diagnosed, Settings › Include my stations' details (off by default) adds their name and address to those reports. Turning it back off stops it immediately.

Album art and song titles

While a station plays, it broadcasts the current song's title. To show cover art, RoRadio looks that title up — first in its own cache (MongoDB Atlas), then at Qwant and Google Programmable Search. Those services receive the song title and the usual technical data of a web request, such as your IP address, under their own privacy policies; they do not receive your installation identifier. Song titles are not attached to diagnostics.

Cloud sync (off until you sign in)

If you turn on user data synchronisation, RoRadio signs you in with the Microsoft account already on your device and stores your favourites, your added stations and your settings in MongoDB Atlas so another device can restore them. The stored record is keyed by a one-way hash of your account identifier: RoRadio does not store your account name, e-mail address or any token. Turn the setting off to stop syncing.

Storage on your device

Favourites, added stations and settings are kept in the app's own storage (on versions of Windows that still roam app data, also in your roaming profile; Windows 11 no longer roams it), not on our servers, unless you turn on cloud sync. Uninstalling RoRadio removes them.

Children

RoRadio is a general-audience app and does not knowingly collect data from children.

Retention and your rights

Diagnostics are kept for as long as Sentry's plan retains them (a matter of weeks to months) and then deleted automatically. Switching Send anonymous diagnostics off is the immediate way to object: the app sends no new reports from that moment, and what was already sent is deleted on that schedule. You may also ask for access, deletion or restriction by writing to the contact address below. The reports carry only a random installation identifier, which the app does not show, so we cannot always tell your reports from other people's; with the app version and roughly when you used it we will look, and where we cannot isolate your reports we can delete the whole group of reports they belong to. You can complain to your national data-protection authority as well.

Changes
